## 🧠 The One Big Idea

Each big platform ships a **design language** with philosophy, components, and guidelines. Following them makes apps feel native and trustworthy (Jakob's Law).

---

## 🟢 Material Design (Google)

- Metaphor of **physical material**: surfaces, elevation, shadows.
- Bold color, motion with meaning, responsive grids.
- **Material 3 (Material You)**: dynamic color, personalization.
- Great for Android + cross-platform web.

## 🍎 Human Interface Guidelines (Apple)

- **Clarity, deference, depth** — content-first, subtle chrome.
- Consistent with iOS/macOS conventions and gestures.
- Emphasis on legibility, translucency, and system components.

## 🔷 Fluent Design (Microsoft)

- **Light, depth, motion, material, scale**.
- Acrylic surfaces, reveal effects; Windows + Microsoft 365.
- Fluent 2 unifies web/desktop/mobile tokens.

---

## ⚖️ Choosing / Blending

| Context | Lean toward |
|---------|-------------|
| Android app | Material |
| iOS/macOS app | HIG |
| Windows/Office tool | Fluent |
| Custom brand web app | Your own system (informed by all) |

Most product teams build their **own** system but borrow patterns and accessibility guidance from these.

---

## 🎯 Key Takeaways

1. Material, HIG, and Fluent are **platform design languages**.
2. **Material** = elevation/material metaphor; **HIG** = clarity/content-first; **Fluent** = light/depth/acrylic.
3. Following them makes apps feel **native**.
4. Match the design language to the **target platform**.
5. Custom systems often **borrow** from all three.
