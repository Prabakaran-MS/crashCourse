# ✏️ Wireframing & Prototyping

> 💼 **Industry Perspective:** In professional frontend teams, **Wireframing & Prototyping** is applied to build reliable, scalable, and maintainable production software. Engineers are expected to understand its trade-offs, best practices, and how it fits into modern architecture, code reviews, and day-to-day team workflows.

> Wireframes and prototypes let you test ideas cheaply before writing code. Fail fast on paper, not in production.

---

## 🧠 The One Big Idea

Increase fidelity gradually: sketch → wireframe → mockup → prototype. Validate at each stage.

---

## 🪜 Fidelity Levels

| Level | What | Purpose |
|-------|------|---------|
| **Sketch** | Pen & paper | Fast idea exploration |
| **Low-fi wireframe** | Boxes, grey blocks | Layout & flow |
| **High-fi mockup** | Real colors, type, content | Visual design |
| **Prototype** | Clickable, interactive | Test flows & usability |

---

## 🛠️ Tools

- **Figma** (industry standard), **Sketch**, **Adobe XD**, **Penpot** (open source).
- Prototyping: linked frames, interactions, components, auto-layout.

---

## 🧪 Usability Testing Prototypes

- Give users **tasks**, observe where they struggle.
- 5 users find ~85% of usability issues.
- Iterate before development.

---

## 🔗 Design-to-Dev

- Use **components + auto-layout** to mirror code structure.
- Annotate spacing, states, and tokens for handoff.

---

## 🎯 Key Takeaways

1. Increase fidelity **gradually**: sketch → wireframe → mockup → prototype.
2. **Low-fi** validates layout/flow cheaply.
3. **Prototypes** enable real usability testing.
4. **Figma** is the industry-standard tool.
5. Test with ~5 users and **iterate before coding**.
